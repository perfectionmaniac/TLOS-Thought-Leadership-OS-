# Knowledge Management

## Purpose

The Knowledge Management skill establishes how the Universal AI Operating Layer acquires, organises, validates, maintains, and applies knowledge.

Its purpose is to ensure that AI reasoning is consistently supported by trustworthy, well-structured, and reusable knowledge rather than isolated information.

Knowledge Management provides the foundation upon which memory, reasoning, recommendations, and workflow execution are built.

---

# Scope

The Knowledge Management skill applies to every form of knowledge used throughout the Universal AI Operating Layer, including organisational knowledge, domain expertise, workflow specifications, policies, reference materials, historical decisions, and learned insights.

It is independent of any specific AI model and provides a common knowledge architecture for all industries and applications.

---

# Core Principle

Reliable intelligence depends on reliable knowledge.

Knowledge should be accurate, structured, current, explainable, and reusable.

---

# Operating Objectives

The Knowledge Management skill exists to:

- acquire knowledge from trusted sources
- organise knowledge into reusable structures
- validate knowledge quality
- maintain knowledge freshness
- preserve organisational learning
- support evidence-based reasoning
- enable efficient knowledge retrieval
- continuously improve the knowledge base

Knowledge may include:

- workflow knowledge

Each category should be managed consistently while preserving its specific context.

# Knowledge Relationships

Knowledge assets should not exist in isolation.

Relationships may include:

- parent knowledge
- supporting knowledge
- dependent knowledge
- related workflows
- associated decisions
- referenced policies

Understanding these relationships improves retrieval, reasoning, and organisational learning.

---

# Knowledge Acquisition

Knowledge should originate from identifiable and appropriate sources.

Typical sources include:

- approved documentation
- verified research
- organisational policies
- historical decisions
- validated analytical outputs
- authorised subject matter experts

Knowledge origin should remain traceable wherever practical.

---

# Knowledge Classification

Every knowledge asset should be classified before use.

Classification may consider:

- domain
- subject
- source
- reliability
- intended use
- confidentiality
- lifecycle status

Consistent classification improves retrieval and governance.

---

# Knowledge Validation

Knowledge should be evaluated before becoming part of the reusable knowledge base.

Validation should consider:

- factual accuracy
- source credibility
- completeness
- consistency
- relevance
- timeliness

Unvalidated knowledge should not be treated as authoritative.

---

# Knowledge Versioning

Knowledge evolves over time.

Significant changes should preserve:

- version history
- revision date
- change rationale
- approval status
- superseded knowledge references

Versioning maintains traceability while supporting continuous improvement.

---

# Knowledge Freshness

Knowledge should remain current.

Periodic review should identify:

- outdated knowledge
- obsolete practices
- superseded standards
- changing regulations
- emerging insights

Knowledge freshness improves decision quality.

---

# Knowledge Retrieval

Knowledge retrieval should prioritise:

- relevance
- reliability
- recency
- contextual suitability
- completeness

Retrieval should minimise unnecessary information while preserving essential context.

---

# Knowledge Application

Knowledge should support reasoning rather than replace analytical thinking.

Applied knowledge should:

- remain relevant to the objective
- support evidence-based conclusions
- acknowledge uncertainty where appropriate
- distinguish facts from assumptions

Knowledge should inform judgement, not substitute it.

---

# Knowledge Governance

Knowledge Management operates under defined governance principles.

Governance includes:

- approved knowledge sources
- validation requirements
- ownership responsibilities
- review schedules
- change management
- archival policies
- retirement of obsolete knowledge

Governance preserves trust in the knowledge ecosystem.

---

# Organisational Learning

Every completed workflow may contribute to organisational knowledge.

Learning may include:

- successful practices
- recurring challenges
- decision outcomes
- validated recommendations
- process improvements

Organisational learning strengthens future reasoning and decision-making.

---

# Human Oversight

Human stakeholders remain responsible for approving significant knowledge changes.

Users should remain able to:

- review knowledge
- correct knowledge
- retire obsolete knowledge
- approve updates
- request validation

Human governance remains authoritative.

---

---

# Capability Boundary

Knowledge Management is responsible for:

- acquiring knowledge
- organising knowledge
- validating knowledge
- maintaining knowledge quality
- supporting retrieval
- preserving organisational learning

Knowledge Management is not responsible for:

- selecting experts
- structuring analytical reasoning
- executing workflows
- generating recommendations
- replacing human governance

Its responsibility is maintaining trustworthy organisational knowledge.

---

# Design Principles

The Knowledge Management skill follows these principles:

1. Knowledge should be trustworthy.
2. Knowledge should remain structured.
3. Knowledge should remain reusable.
4. Knowledge should evolve through controlled improvement.
5. Knowledge should remain traceable.
6. Governance should preserve quality.
7. Organisational learning should strengthen future intelligence.
8. Human oversight remains authoritative.
